# ENVE 101 — Chapter 7: Climate Change and Green Engineering
**Kathmandu University | Exam Study Priority**

> **Priority basis:** This is a study-priority guide made from the official ENVE 101 (2026) syllabus plus the amount of emphasis, formulas, worked examples, tables, and diagrams in the uploaded Kathmandu University lecture PDF. It is **not an official prediction of exam questions**.

**Priority key:**  
- **A — Highest:** explicit syllabus item + strongly developed in the lecture / numerical or major diagram  
- **B — Important:** explicit syllabus item or substantial lecture topic  
- **C — Review:** supporting material, examples, or background


## A — Highest Priority

### 1. Carbon Cycle
Know:
- carbon cycle as a major biogeochemical cycle;
- movement of carbon between environment and living organisms;
- role of photosynthesis;
- terrestrial plants and ocean phytoplankton;
- ocean uptake and carbon sequestration as bicarbonate/calcium carbonate;
- weathering returning carbon to accessible forms.
- **PDF:** pp. 2–5.

Be able to explain the carbon-cycle diagram and distinguish **carbon stocks** from **carbon flows**.

### 2. Climate Change
Definition in the lecture:
- long-term alteration of Earth’s weather patterns, including temperature, rainfall, wind and storms.

Signs listed:
- rising global temperature;
- melting glaciers/ice caps;
- rising sea level.
- **PDF:** p. 6.

### 3. Greenhouse Effect
Know:
- greenhouse-effect concept;
- human activities: fossil-fuel burning, deforestation, industrial emissions;
- greenhouse gases listed in the lecture: `CO₂`, `CH₄`, `O₃`, etc.;
- heat trapping and increased surface temperature.
- **PDF:** pp. 7–8.

### 4. Climate-Change Mitigation
Definition and examples:
- reduce/prevent greenhouse-gas emissions;
- new technologies;
- renewable energy;
- improve equipment efficiency;
- changes in practices/consumer behavior.
- **PDF:** p. 9.

### 5. Climate Vulnerability
Page 10 emphasizes why poorer countries/populations can face greater climate threats:
- limited physical, financial and human resources for adaptation;
- location in highly affected areas;
- exposure of poor populations;
- dependence on climate-sensitive sectors such as agriculture and tourism.
- **PDF:** p. 10.

### 6. Climate Finance
Know:
- definition;
- purpose;
- funders/sources;
- importance;
- public vs private sources;
- Nepal context.
- **PDF:** pp. 11–14.

Applications listed include:
- renewable energy;
- climate-resilient infrastructure;
- forest conservation/restoration;
- water-resource management;
- disaster preparedness/risk reduction.

### 7. Twelve Principles of Green Engineering
Memorize the 12 lecture principles:
1. Inherent Rather Than Circumstantial
2. Prevention Instead of Treatment
3. Design for Separation
4. Maximize Efficiency
5. Output-Pulled Versus Input-Pushed
6. Conserve Complexity
7. Durability Rather Than Immortality
8. Meet Need, Minimize Excess
9. Minimize Material Diversity
10. Integrate Material and Energy Flows
11. Design for Commercial Afterlife
12. Renewable Rather Than Depleting
- **PDF:** p. 15.

### 8. Green-Engineering Design Strategy Figure
Understand the hierarchy in the p. 16 figure:
- optimize existing solution;
- reengineer the system;
- redefine the problem.

The figure relates increasing intervention/investment to potential benefits.
- **PDF:** p. 16.
