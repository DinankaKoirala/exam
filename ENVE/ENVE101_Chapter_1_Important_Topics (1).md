## A — Highest Priority

### 1. Engineering and Environmental Engineering
- Definitions of **engineering** and **environmental engineering**.
- Main scope of environmental engineering:
  - water, soil, air and noise pollution control;
  - water and wastewater treatment;
  - solid-waste collection, transport and disposal;
  - environmental assessment;
  - environmental input to development decisions.
- **PDF:** pp. 8–9.

### 2. Engineering Decisions
Know the four required considerations:
1. **Technically feasible**
2. **Economically viable**
3. **Environmentally friendly**
4. **Socially acceptable**

Also review technical feasibility, cost effectiveness/IRR, environmental analysis and social analysis.  
- **PDF:** pp. 14–22.

### 3. Units, Dimensions, Prefixes and Scale
- Difference between **value, unit and dimension**.
- SI prefixes from pico to giga.
- Unit conversion and size/scale questions.
- **PDF:** pp. 25–28.

### 4. Density and Concentration
Know:
- `ρ = M/V`
- concentration as mass of solute per total solution volume;
- `mg/L`, `kg/m³`, `ppm`, `ppb`;
- for water/soil examples, conversions used in the lecture.
- **PDF:** pp. 29–35.

### 5. Air Concentration and Ideal-Gas Relation
- `ppmv = (V_i/V_total) × 10^6`
- `ppmv = (moles_i/moles_total) × 10^6`
- `PV = nRT`
- effect of temperature and pressure on gas concentration conversion.
- **PDF:** pp. 32–35.

### 6. Flow Rate and Mass Loading
Must know:
- `Q_V = V/t`
- common units: `L/s`, `L/day`, `MLD`, `m³/s`
- `1 m³ = 1000 L`
- `1 MLD = 10^6 L/day`
- mass loading: `Q_L = C × Q_V`
- treatment/removal-efficiency type calculations.
- **PDF:** pp. 36–41.

### 7. Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT)
Definition and derivation:
- `HRT = V/Q`
- also called detention time / residence time.
- **PDF:** pp. 42–44.

### 8. Approximations in Engineering Calculations
- Why engineers use approximations.
- Making defensible assumptions from limited information.
- Order-of-magnitude / tentative cost estimation.
- **PDF:** pp. 45–50.
