# ENGT-105 Unit I: Technical Communication Basics

## What is Technical Communication?

Technical communication is any form of communication that:
1. Covers technical or specialized topics (e.g. medical procedures, software, regulations)
2. Provides instructions on how to do something
3. Is delivered *through* technology (web pages, social media, help files)

It is a **user-centered approach** — delivering the right information, in the right way, at the right time to make the user's work more productive.

### Key Characteristics
- Clear, concise, factual
- Audience-centered and purpose-driven
- Structured for professional/practical contexts
- Written and oral; for supervisors, colleagues, vendors, clients

---

## The Five Goals / Objectives of Technical Communication

| Goal | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Information must be immediately understandable |
| **Conciseness** | No unnecessary words or detail |
| **Accuracy** | Facts, data, figures must be correct |
| **Organization** | Logical structure so readers can follow |
| **Ethics** | Honest, unbiased, legally compliant |

### Achieving Clarity — 4 Methods
1. **Provide Specific Details** — say *exactly* what you mean (avoid vague words like "soon", "many", "negative impact")
2. **Answer the Reporter's Questions** — Who? What? When? Where? Why? How?
3. **Use Easily Understandable Words** — avoid obscure or overly technical vocabulary for the audience
4. **Use Active Voice** — "John broke the machine" not "The machine was broken by John"

## Audience Recognition

Your audience is whoever reads your memo, email, report, blog, or web page.

### Types of Audience

| Type | Who They Are | What They Need |
|---|---|---|
| **High-Tech** | Experts in your field; same educational background | No jargon explanation; minimal background |
| **Low-Tech** | Colleagues in other departments; familiar but peripheral | Define technical concepts; some background |
| **Lay** | Customers/clients with no expertise | Simple language, no jargon, clear graphics, full background |

### Questions to Ask Before Writing
- Who is your audience?
- What do they already know? What don't they know?
- What is their position relative to yours?
- What diversity/multicultural issues should you consider?
- How do you communicate with more than one audience type simultaneously?

## The Communication Process

A **message** is sent by a **sender** → through a **communication channel** → to a **receiver**.

Misunderstanding can occur at *any* stage. Effective communication minimizes this.

### Communication Channels
The *method* used to transmit a message. Chosen based on:
- Audience
- Purpose
- Situation

Examples: email, phone call, memo, report, presentation, social media, web page

---

## The Writing Process (Prewrite → Write → Rewrite)

### 1. Prewrite
Understand needs before writing:
- **Examine purpose** — external (someone asked you) or internal (you decided to write)
- **Determine goals** — inform, instruct, persuade, or build trust
- **Consider audience** — type, diversity, multicultural needs
- **Gather data** — answer reporter's questions OR use mind mapping

#### Four Goals of Communication
| Goal | Example |
|---|---|
| Inform | Email announcing a staff meeting |
| Instruct | Memo directing staff on form-filling |
| Persuade | Progress report justifying a raise |
| Build trust | Thanking a co-worker for their help |

#### Reporter's Questions vs. Mind Mapping
| Reporter's Questions | Mind Mapping |
|---|---|
| Structured: Who, What, When, Where, Why, How | Free-form, branching idea diagram |
| Best for factual, focused topics | Best for brainstorming broad topics |
| Gets specific details quickly | Reveals connections between ideas |

### 2. Write
- Organize gathered information
- Add visual effects (tables, graphics)
- Format the content appropriately

### 3. Rewrite
- The most essential stage
- Edit for clarity, accuracy, tone
- Test usability of the document

---

## Document Design
Effective design supports communication. Key principles:
- Use headers and subheadings for navigation
- Use white space — don't crowd text
- Use bullet lists for parallel items
- Use tables for comparative data
- Graphics/visuals must support the text, not decorate it

---

## Common Barriers to Effective Communication
1. Disinterest or dissatisfaction with one's job
2. Inability to actively listen
3. Assumptions about the receiver's knowledge
4. Inappropriate channel choice
5. Cultural/diversity mismatches
