# ENVE 101 — Chapter 6: Environmental Management System
**Kathmandu University | Exam Study Priority**

> **Priority basis:** This is a study-priority guide made from the official ENVE 101 (2026) syllabus plus the amount of emphasis, formulas, worked examples, tables, and diagrams in the uploaded Kathmandu University lecture PDF. It is **not an official prediction of exam questions**.

**Priority key:**  
- **A — Highest:** explicit syllabus item + strongly developed in the lecture / numerical or major diagram  
- **B — Important:** explicit syllabus item or substantial lecture topic  
- **C — Review:** supporting material, examples, or background


## A — Highest Priority

### 1. Environmental Management and EMS
Know:
- definition of environmental management;
- definition and purpose of an **Environmental Management System (EMS)**;
- reducing environmental impacts and improving operating efficiency.
- **PDF:** p. 2.

### 2. ISO 14001
Know that ISO 14001 is the internationally recognized EMS standard and the elements shown in the lecture:
- environmental policy;
- planning;
- implementation & operation;
- monitoring & corrective action;
- management review;
- continuous improvement.
- **PDF:** p. 3.

### 3. PDCA Cycle
Be able to draw and explain:
- **Plan:** identify impacts, set objectives, prepare action plans.
- **Do:** implement, train, follow procedures.
- **Check:** monitor, measure, audit.
- **Act:** corrective action, improve performance, update policy/plans.
- **PDF:** p. 4.

### 4. Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)
Know:
- purpose of LCA;
- product substitution/comparison;
- **cradle-to-grave** idea: raw materials → manufacture → use → transport → waste management.
- **PDF:** pp. 5–8.

### 5. Four Stages of LCA
Memorize and explain:
1. Goal and scope definition
2. Inventory analysis
3. Impact assessment
4. Interpretation
- **PDF:** pp. 9–10.

### 6. Functional Unit (FU)
Definition:
- a quantified reference for product-system performance used to make alternatives comparable.

Be able to identify a suitable FU in a comparison.
- **PDF:** pp. 13–19.

**Important reasoning point:** the plate example initially divides annual servings by lifetime uses, but p. 19 asks whether a café can operate with only `5` steel plates and states that at least `125` plates are needed to serve lunch each day. Review pp. 16–19 carefully.

### 7. Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
Know the definition and functions:
- identify likely environmental/social/economic/health impacts;
- evaluate significance;
- determine mitigation;
- recommend preventive/corrective measures;
- inform decision-makers;
- advise whether development should proceed.
- **PDF:** pp. 20–21.

### 8. EIA and Project Cycle
Study the project-cycle diagram and where EIA integrates into it.
- **PDF:** p. 22.

### 9. Environmental Laws/Regulations in Nepal
Names given in the lecture:
- Environment Protection Act (EPA), 2076;
- Environmental Protection Rules (EPR), 2077;
- Solid Waste Management Act, 2068;
- Water Resources Act, 2049;
- relevant policies/standards/guidelines.
- **PDF:** p. 23.

### 10. BES vs IEE vs EIA in Nepal
Know the **three levels of environmental study**, their purpose by project size and the lecture examples.
- **BES:** p. 24.
- **IEE:** p. 25.
- **EIA:** p. 26.

Examples worth remembering from the lecture include:
- hospital/hotel thresholds;
- road/bridge examples;
- forest-area acquisition;
- solar/hydropower thresholds.
